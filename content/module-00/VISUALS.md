# Module 00 — Visual Explainers

## Visual 1 — Three dimensions, one decision system

**Purpose:** Show that E, S and G are analytical dimensions rather than independent workstreams.

**Elements:** A central “Business decision” node connects to three rings. Environmental nodes: climate, energy, water, materials, nature, waste. Social nodes: workforce, supply-chain workers, customers, communities, rights and wellbeing. Governance nodes: authority, evidence, controls, incentives, challenge and accountability. Cross-links show that a material choice can affect extraction, workers, delivery resilience and supplier approval.

**Labels:** “Depends on,” “Affects,” “Creates uncertainty for,” “Controlled by.”

**Learner takeaway:** One decision can carry several ESG dimensions, and governance determines whether the organization sees and manages them.

**Proposed interaction:** Select any node to highlight upstream dependencies, downstream impacts and governance controls. A text alternative lists the same relationships.

## Visual 2 — Impact–dependency–risk–opportunity chain

**Purpose:** Translate abstract ESG vocabulary into a causal model.

**Elements:** Two-direction flow: organization → impact → environmental/social condition; condition → dependency → operational exposure. Both feed a decision diamond labeled “Risk or opportunity?” followed by action, measure and review.

**Labels:** “What we change,” “What we rely on,” “What could affect objectives,” “What could improve the outcome.”

**Learner takeaway:** Impact and dependency are not synonyms for risk; they are pathways through which risk or opportunity can arise.

**Proposed interaction:** Learners switch between water, skilled labor and supplier integrity examples. No scoring; the visual prepares them for the activity.

## Visual 3 — Governance failure cascade

**Purpose:** Explain the case logic of Enron without reproducing copyrighted imagery or dramatizing individuals.

**Elements:** Pressure/incentives → opaque transactions → conflicted assurance → suppressed warning → misleading information → delayed intervention → organizational and stakeholder consequences. Barrier icons indicate where independent challenge, transparent reporting, audit integrity or speak-up protection could interrupt the chain.

**Labels:** “Driver,” “Control weakness,” “Warning,” “Decision failure,” “Consequence.”

**Learner takeaway:** Major failure is often produced by interacting weaknesses, not one isolated bad act.

**Proposed interaction:** Learners activate proposed controls and see which pathway they interrupt. The visual must state that controls reduce risk; they do not guarantee prevention.
