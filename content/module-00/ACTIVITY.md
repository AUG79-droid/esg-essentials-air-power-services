# Module 00 — Interactive Activity: ESG Compass

## Scenario

An Air Power Services team must recommend a supplier for a support component. Option North has the lowest price and shortest lead time but provides limited sub-tier evidence. Option East has higher cost and a longer lead time, along with material traceability and stronger workforce and control information. A third option is to retain the current supplier while requiring a corrective evidence plan.

This is an **AIR POWER SERVICES LEARNING SCENARIO**. It is fictional, non-classified and not based on Airbus policy or an actual supplier.

## Learner task

Build a decision map before choosing an option. The learner must classify evidence signals, connect causes and consequences, identify missing evidence and propose one governance condition.

## Choices and actions

1. Place twelve cards under any relevant lenses: environmental, social, governance, operational/financial. Multiple placement is allowed.
2. Connect at least four causal links—for example, “unverified subcontracting” → “unknown labor/material conditions” → “delivery and rights risk.”
3. Mark each signal as claim, evidence, assumption or unknown.
4. Select an initial recommendation: choose North, choose East, retain current supplier with conditions, or defer/escalate.
5. Add two conditions or information requests before finalizing.

## Feedback

Feedback evaluates reasoning rather than one prescribed supplier choice.

- If the learner forces every card into one pillar, feedback explains cross-pillar relationships.
- If price and schedule are ignored, feedback explains that ESG does not replace commercial or operational evidence.
- If a badge is treated as proof, feedback asks for scope, methodology, date and independent validation.
- If the learner recommends indefinite delay, feedback asks which uncertainty is material and what proportionate action could resolve it.
- If severe rights or integrity indicators are traded against aggregate benefits, feedback requires escalation and protection before score balancing.

## Learning logic

The activity teaches that ESG is a structured inquiry. A defensible decision states objectives, affected parties, dependencies, impacts, evidence, uncertainty, controls and ownership. Different recommendations can be reasonable when assumptions are explicit and non-negotiable requirements are respected.

## Expected outcome

The learner produces a connected map and a conditional recommendation such as: “Do not approve solely on price and schedule. Obtain sub-tier scope and traceability evidence, validate the audit boundary and define monitoring/escalation conditions. Compare the residual risk and lifecycle consequence of all feasible options.”
