# Module 06 — Visual Explainers

## Visual 1 — Governance chain

**Purpose:** Make `OBJECTIVE → RISK → CONTROL → EVIDENCE → MONITORING → ESCALATION → CORRECTIVE ACTION` operational.  
**Elements:** Connected nodes with feedback from corrective action to objective/risk; owner and authority lane below.  
**Takeaway:** Missing links create false confidence.  
**Interaction:** Learners place programme artifacts into the chain and identify gaps.

## Visual 2 — Authority and assurance map

**Purpose:** Distinguish operational ownership, specialist challenge and independent assurance.  
**Elements:** Board/senior oversight, management, technical authority, operations, risk, compliance, quality and internal audit; arrows labeled decide, execute, advise/challenge, assure and escalate.  
**Takeaway:** Participation in a review does not mean every participant approved every dimension.  
**Interaction:** Select a decision to reveal required authority. No universal structure is implied.

## Visual 3 — Control effectiveness matrix

**Purpose:** Separate design, execution and effectiveness.  
**Elements:** Columns for risk addressed, design fit, evidence of operation, outcome/exception trend, owner and response.  
**Takeaway:** A completed checklist can coexist with ineffective control.  
**Interaction:** Compare three controls and diagnose the failure type.

## Visual 4 — Warning-to-escalation pathway

**Purpose:** Show how information can be diluted.  
**Elements:** Raw warning → classification → dashboard → management review → authority → action; branches show buried, downgraded, delayed and conflicted information.  
**Takeaway:** Information governance is part of control.  
**Interaction:** Restore the warning and observe which decisions change.

## Visual 5 — Risk appetite and non-compensable constraints

**Purpose:** Distinguish governed risk choices from mandatory gates.  
**Elements:** Capacity → appetite → tolerance → operational limit/trigger; a separate gate lane for safety, airworthiness, security, legal prohibition, mandatory technical authority and severe rights constraints.  
**Takeaway:** Weighted ESG benefit cannot compensate for failure of a mandatory gate.  
**Interaction:** Learner removes infeasible options, compares remaining choices and routes exceptions to the correct escalation authority.
