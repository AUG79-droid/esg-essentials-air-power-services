# Module 09 — Air Power Services ESG Decision Lab

## Capstone opening

An urgent in-service avionics and equipment retrofit is required to maintain operational readiness. The approved component is constrained. A proposed alternative changes supplier, material, chemical treatment and logistics. Evidence is incomplete, schedule pressure is rising and the KPIs do not all point in the same direction.

This capstone does not ask for the “greenest” choice. It asks for a responsible, evidence-based and governed decision.

Safety, airworthiness, security and technical authority are **non-negotiable constraints**. ESG does not override them. ESG information belongs inside the decision while those constraints remain mandatory.

**Expected learning time:** 35 minutes

### Learning objectives

You will integrate environmental, social and governance evidence; distinguish facts, claims and unknowns; identify affected stakeholders and rights-holders; trace lifecycle and supply-chain consequences; apply authority and escalation; compare options without a single ESG score; and document monitoring and residual uncertainty.

## Scenario frame

This is an **AIR POWER SERVICES LEARNING SCENARIO**. It is fictional, non-classified and does not represent Airbus policy, an actual programme, actual aircraft configuration, actual supplier or technical instruction.

The programme must address obsolescence in an avionics support unit used within a wider equipment retrofit. Four pathways remain conceptually available:

1. continue limited repair while inventory lasts;
2. redesign around an available component through the incumbent supplier;
3. qualify a new supplier offering shorter lead time and lower price;
4. stage a controlled interim solution while evidence and technical qualification progress.

The learner receives information in stages. Earlier choices determine which evidence becomes available, how much schedule flexibility remains and whether stakeholders trust the process. No route can bypass mandatory technical gates.

## How the lab works

The lab evaluates nine dimensions qualitatively:

- evidence quality;
- systems thinking;
- risk recognition;
- stakeholder and rights-holder awareness;
- governance discipline;
- uncertainty management;
- escalation quality;
- lifecycle thinking;
- decision traceability.

Feedback uses `STRONG`, `DEVELOPING` and `NEEDS REVIEW`. These levels support learning; they do not certify competence, establish legal expertise or authorize corporate decisions.

## Decision lens

Before every stage, ask:

1. What decision is actually available at this point?
2. Which authority and non-negotiable constraints apply?
3. What is fact, claim, estimate, indicator or unknown?
4. Who or what is affected across the lifecycle and value chain?
5. Could this choice reduce one risk while moving harm elsewhere?
6. What evidence would materially change the decision?
7. What condition, control or escalation makes the option defensible?
8. What must be monitored after approval?

## Capstone close

The final decision is not the end of ESG responsibility. It creates assumptions and conditions that must be monitored. A responsible decision can remain uncertain; it cannot conceal uncertainty or bypass authority.

ESG is not a label attached after a decision. It is part of how evidence, impacts, risks, people and governance are considered **before and during** the decision.
