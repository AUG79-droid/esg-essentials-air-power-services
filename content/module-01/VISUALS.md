# Module 01 — Visual Explainers

## Visual 1 — Scope boundary map

**Purpose:** Explain Scopes 1–3 without implying a current legal reporting requirement.  
**Elements:** Technical maintenance facility at center; owned combustion/process sources, purchased electricity, upstream materials/logistics, business travel, downstream use/end-of-life.  
**Labels:** “Direct source,” “Purchased energy,” “Other value-chain activity,” “Boundary/method matters.”  
**Takeaway:** Scopes locate emissions; they do not prove data quality or assign a simple value judgment.  
**Interaction:** Learners classify activities, then reveal estimation and double-counting caveats. A dated note identifies the GHG Protocol documents used and warns that the Corporate Suite is under revision.

## Visual 2 — Environmental dependency and impact map

**Purpose:** Show two-way relationships.  
**Elements:** Operational node connected to energy, water, materials, land/nature and waste systems; arrows enter for dependencies and leave for impacts; risk/opportunity cards attach to pathways.  
**Labels:** “We rely on,” “We change,” “This can affect objectives.”  
**Takeaway:** A topic can be both a dependency and an impact.  
**Interaction:** Learner selects a maintenance, supplier or facility scenario and builds paths.

## Visual 3 — Climate risk pathway

**Purpose:** Prevent climate risk from being treated as hazard alone.  
**Elements:** Hazard → exposure → vulnerability → impact → adaptation response → residual risk; acute/chronic toggle; assumptions and monitoring loop; mitigation shown as a separate response to climate drivers.  
**Takeaway:** Risk depends on what is exposed and how susceptible it is; adaptation changes but rarely eliminates risk.  
**Interaction:** Adjust exposure or vulnerability controls, compare two scenarios and reveal residual risk and uncertainty without fake probability values.

## Visual 4 — Circular lifecycle with leakage

**Purpose:** Compare value-preserving loops.  
**Elements:** Materials → manufacture → use/maintenance → repair → reuse → remanufacture → recycle → disposal; energy, transport and quality-control inputs shown at each loop.  
**Takeaway:** A circular label is insufficient; loops carry technical and environmental trade-offs.  
**Interaction:** Select a loop to reveal preserved value, new inputs, evidence needs and failure modes.

## Visual 5 — Nature dependency–impact–risk map

**Purpose:** Make nature risk broader than biodiversity counting.  
**Elements:** Locate → dependency/impact pathways → risk/opportunity → response; location card for ecosystem condition, basin and affected rights-holders.  
**Takeaway:** Nature dependencies and impacts are location-sensitive and can transmit into operational and social consequences.  
**Interaction:** Select a fictional facility or supplier and trace one dependency and one impact through the LEAP-inspired screen.

## Visual 6 — LCA system boundary and comparison basis

**Purpose:** Show why a benefit in one stage does not prove lower total impact.  
**Elements:** Functional unit above two alternative lifecycle chains; boundary boxes, included/excluded stages, inventory arrows, multiple impact-category lanes, allocation and uncertainty flags.  
**Takeaway:** Comparison is only meaningful with aligned function, boundary, assumptions and data quality.  
**Interaction:** Toggle a transport, use-life or end-of-life assumption and observe whether the decision remains robust—without calculating a fake LCA score.

## Visual 7 — Pollution pathway and control hierarchy

**Purpose:** Connect hazard, release, exposure and controls while preserving technical gates.  
**Elements:** Substance/function → release pathway → receptor → consequence; eliminate/substitute → contain → process control → monitor → emergency/corrective action; safety/airworthiness/security/qualification gate across all options.  
**Takeaway:** A safer alternative must still perform its function and pass mandatory authority.

## Visual rule

No aircraft is combined with forest, leaves, green landscape or planet symbolism. If a later technical aircraft view is authorized, only A400M or Eurofighter may appear and only in a hangar, factory, maintenance, engineering, air-base, digital-twin or technical-operations context. These explainers should primarily use schematic industrial systems rather than aircraft imagery.
