# Module 01 — Interactive Activity: Environmental Dependency & Impact Map

## Scenario

An **AIR POWER SERVICES LEARNING SCENARIO** presents a component-life-extension decision across repair, remanufacture and replacement. All options are technically feasible; the dataset deliberately contains gaps.

## Learner task

Build and defend an environmental system map rather than selecting the option with the most attractive single metric.

## Actions

1. Define the comparison’s function, duration and system boundary; mark exclusions.
2. Place energy, water, material, pollution, nature and waste cards as dependencies, impacts or both.
3. Connect at least five transmission pathways to cost, continuity, worker/community effect or resilience.
4. Inspect evidence cards and mark measured, estimated, claim or unknown.
5. Select an option, request more evidence or recommend a controlled pilot.
6. Add two monitoring conditions and a review trigger.
7. Test one climate hazard through hazard → exposure → vulnerability → adaptation → residual risk.
8. Change one lifecycle assumption and decide whether the recommendation remains robust.

## Feedback

- Choosing “lowest operational energy” alone triggers lifecycle/burden-shifting feedback.
- Choosing “repair” solely because it avoids new material reveals transport, repeated process and quality-control inputs.
- Treating global water volume as sufficient reveals location/season/quality limitations.
- Ignoring readiness or safety requires the learner to restore them as constraints.
- Demanding perfect data prompts proportionate uncertainty management.

## Learning logic

The interaction rewards coherent boundaries, causal pathways, evidence discipline and controls. It does not calculate a universal environmental score.

## Expected outcome

A defensible conditional recommendation that identifies the most material environmental pathways, limits of evidence and how actual outcomes will be tested.
