# Module 04 — Visual Explainers

## Visual 1 — Social stakeholder and rights-holder map

**Purpose:** Show the breadth of Social.  
**Elements:** Employees, contractors, supply-chain workers, customers/users, communities, civil society and regulators around a decision; rights-holder markers where specific protections apply.  
**Takeaway:** “Stakeholder” is broad, but rights cannot be reduced to preferences.  
**Interaction:** Select a decision to reveal effect, evidence and voice channel for each group.

## Visual 2 — Equality, equity and accessibility decision flow

**Purpose:** Avoid simplistic imagery and connect fairness to valid standards.  
**Elements:** Define valid requirement → identify barriers → provide accessible/fair route → measure participation and outcome → review unintended effects.  
**Takeaway:** Equity changes barriers and access, not legitimate competence or safety requirements.  
**Interaction:** Apply the flow to training, PPE or facilities.

## Visual 3 — Social evidence stack

**Purpose:** Prevent reliance on one representation number.  
**Elements:** Context → input → process → experience → outcome → grievance/remedy → governance; privacy shield across all layers.  
**Takeaway:** Evidence must be triangulated and interpreted in context.  
**Interaction:** Learners identify what a metric can and cannot establish.

## Visual 4 — Stakeholder and rights-holder engagement cycle

**Purpose:** Distinguish consultation from one-way communication.  
**Elements:** Identify → understand impact → design accessible/safe engagement → engage → respond → feedback closure → monitor; representation and power checks at each step.  
**Takeaway:** Rights are not preferences, and engagement must be early enough to influence a decision.  
**Interaction:** Learner repairs a fictional engagement plan that excludes a less visible affected group.

## Visual 5 — Input-to-impact indicator chain

**Purpose:** Prevent activities and outputs from being reported as outcomes or impacts.  
**Elements:** Input → activity → output → outcome → impact, with leading/lagging tags, denominator, boundary, privacy and perverse-incentive checks.  
**Takeaway:** The further the claim moves toward impact, the stronger the causal and contextual evidence required.
