# Module 05 — Visual Explainers

## Visual 1 — Multi-tier risk network

**Purpose:** Show why contractual tier and actual risk pathway differ.  
**Elements:** Buyer, tier-one assembler, processor, mineral/refiner, electronics sub-tier, labor recruiter, logistics, outsourced service and affected community. Contract links, material flows, labor flows and evidence flows use separate line styles.  
**Labels:** “Known relationship,” “Unverified sub-tier,” “Labor intermediary,” “Evidence gap,” “Potential severe-impact pathway.”  
**Learner takeaway:** Supplier mapping is not proof of control; missing visibility should be prioritized by potential harm.  
**Interaction:** Reveal tiers progressively and let the learner trace which evidence reaches each node.

## Visual 2 — Two-lens risk matrix

**Purpose:** Distinguish risk to people from risk to company.  
**Elements:** Parallel panels. People lens: scale, scope, irremediability and vulnerability. Company lens: cost, schedule, continuity, legal/reputation and strategic exposure. Shared causal links connect without collapsing the panels.  
**Takeaway:** Severe harm may require action even when immediate enterprise exposure is low.  
**Interaction:** Sort scenario evidence into one or both lenses; severe people-risk cannot be averaged away.

## Visual 3 — Evidence triangulation ladder

**Purpose:** Compare strength and limitation of evidence.  
**Elements:** Policy/claim, certificate, audit report, operational records, observation, protected worker voice, corrective-action outcome and remedy evidence. This is a ladder of questions, not a universal ranking.  
**Takeaway:** Multiple independent sources improve confidence; each can still have limitations.  
**Interaction:** Selecting an item reveals scope, incentive, recency and safeguarding questions.

## Visual 4 — Due-diligence and remedy cycle

**Purpose:** Show ongoing process rather than one audit.  
**Elements:** Embed/map → identify and assess → prioritise by severity to people → prevent/mitigate → track → communicate → provide for/cooperate in remedy → learn. Purchasing-practice and rights-holder engagement cross the entire cycle.  
**Takeaway:** Closing an audit finding is not the same as remedying harm.  
**Interaction:** Learners place actions in the cycle and see feedback when affected-person engagement, remedy or people-risk prioritization is omitted.

## Visual 5 — Responsible-purchasing causal chain

**Purpose:** Reveal how buyer decisions can contribute to supplier risk.  
**Elements:** Forecast/specification/price/payment/lead time → supplier capacity response → working-condition pathway → indicator → joint prevention action.  
**Takeaway:** Supplier requirements and buyer incentives must not contradict each other.  
**Interaction:** Adjust a fictional lead time or late change and select a governed response without relaxing technical requirements.

## Visual 6 — Grievance effectiveness loop

**Purpose:** Show that a channel is not an effective mechanism by itself.  
**Elements:** Access → safe intake → assessment/escalation → response → remedy → feedback closure → learning; trust, non-retaliation, confidentiality and records surround the loop.  
**Takeaway:** Case closure does not prove resolution or remedy.  
**Interaction:** Diagnose barriers and test the eight UNGP effectiveness criteria.

## Visual 7 — Community-impact pathway

**Purpose:** Connect activity to differentiated community effects.  
**Elements:** Activity → environmental/social pathway → affected groups/vulnerability → cumulative effect → mitigation → monitoring/grievance.  
**Takeaway:** One representative or legal permit does not establish meaningful engagement or fair impact distribution.

## Visual policy

Use dark navy network/control-room styling with cyan evidence flows and amber uncertainty. Do not use aircraft-with-nature imagery or imply that aerospace products are environmentally or socially beneficial by default.
