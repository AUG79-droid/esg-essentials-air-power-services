# Module 05 — Interactive Activity: Supply Chain Investigation

## Scenario

The **AIR POWER SERVICES LEARNING SCENARIO** concerns an urgent electronics module. The learner receives incomplete evidence for four tiers: technical qualification status, supplier audit, payroll/time records, recruitment agreement, worker-channel data, mineral traceability, delivery requirements and buyer forecast changes.

## Learner task

Construct an investigation view without reaching beyond the evidence.

## Choices and actions

1. Classify each card: `KNOWN`, `UNKNOWN`, `RISK INDICATOR`, `ALLEGATION`, `VERIFIED HARM`, `CONTROL EVIDENCE` or `CONFLICTING EVIDENCE`.
2. Map affected rights-holders and enterprise objectives separately.
3. Select five evidence requests and explain why each is proportionate.
4. Identify two buyer practices that may contribute to pressure.
5. Choose immediate action for each issue: continue monitoring, clarify, protect/escalate, investigate, prevent/mitigate or consider responsible disengagement.
6. Write two statements that must **not** be concluded from the evidence.
7. Design a safe grievance-response loop and distinguish process correction from remedy to affected workers.

## Feedback

- Calling inconsistent overtime “proof of forced labor” prompts the distinction between indicator and verified harm.
- Treating a passed audit as proof of safety reveals scope, timing, interview and subcontracting limits.
- Choosing only corporate-risk evidence requires a rights-holder severity assessment.
- Requesting worker names or public interviews triggers confidentiality and retaliation safeguards.
- Recommending abrupt exit reveals potential harm to workers and loss of leverage.
- Ignoring buyer lead-time changes reveals causal contribution.
- Waiting for perfect evidence when severe indicators exist requires proportionate protective escalation.
- Closing the case after changing a policy reveals that stopping harm, preventing recurrence, correcting process and remedying people are distinct.

## Learning logic

The activity rewards disciplined classification, rights-holder awareness, evidence triangulation, contribution analysis and proportionate escalation. Technical approval is shown on a separate mandatory track: the learner cannot substitute social reasoning for safety, security, airworthiness or technical authority.

## Expected outcome

A strong learner preserves uncertainty, prioritizes potential severity to people, requests protected and decision-relevant evidence, examines buyer causation and recommends a governed next step rather than a premature verdict.
