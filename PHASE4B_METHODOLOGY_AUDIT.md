## Methodological additions

### Materiality and double materiality

- An eight-step framework-neutral process now covers purpose/scope, context, candidate issues, evidence, significance, criteria/thresholds, validation/governance, documentation and review.
- Impact materiality and financial materiality are assessed separately but connected.
- Severity to people/environment uses scale, scope and irremediable character, with likelihood for potential impacts.
- Financial assessment considers nature/magnitude, timing and likelihood of possible effects on organizational prospects.
- Either lens may make an issue material in a double-materiality context; the method does not require both.
- ESRS/EFRAG, GRI and ISSB purposes are distinguished. EFRAG IG 1 is identified as non-authoritative implementation guidance; ISSB is not relabelled double materiality.

### Climate, nature, pollution and lifecycle

- Physical risk follows hazard → exposure → vulnerability → impact → adaptation response → residual risk, with acute/chronic hazards, scenario assumptions, uncertainty and monitoring.
- Nature uses dependencies, impacts, risks and opportunities with TNFD LEAP-inspired location sensitivity; biodiversity counting alone is rejected.
- Pollution prevention follows hazard/function, release/exposure pathways, applicable controls, technically feasible substitution, containment/process control, monitoring and emergency/corrective action.
- Lifecycle thinking is separated from formal LCA. Functional unit, boundary, stages, inventory, impact categories, allocation, data quality, burden shifting, uncertainty and interpretation are explained.
- Safety, airworthiness, security and technical qualification remain gates across all environmental decisions.

### Data and assurance

- The evidence model is source → owner → definition → boundary → method → calculation → control → review → change history.
- Estimates are allowed when labelled, controlled and accompanied by uncertainty; false precision is rejected.
- Internal review, internal audit, verification, certification and external assurance are distinguished.
- Assurance readiness covers criteria, boundary, method, evidence, controls, review, reproducibility, traceability and correction; it does not guarantee a conclusion.

### Human rights, people and engagement

- HRDD follows identify → assess → prioritise → prevent/mitigate → track → communicate → enable remedy, with cause/contribute/directly-linked response logic.
- Severity to people can take priority even where financial exposure is initially low.
- Remedy is separated from stopping harm, preventing recurrence and correcting process.
- Responsible purchasing tests specification changes, lead time, volume/forecasting, price/payment and buyer leverage.
- Worker interviewing covers voluntary participation, privacy, independence, power imbalance, confidentiality limits, secure records, follow-up and retaliation risk at awareness level.
- Grievance effectiveness uses the UNGP criteria and a complete access-to-learning loop.
- Stakeholders and rights-holders are distinguished; consultation is not one-way notification.
- Community screening covers affected groups, impact pathways, vulnerability, cumulative effects, evidence, uncertainty, mitigation, monitoring and feedback.
- People analytics covers purpose limitation, minimisation, aggregation, access, retention, confidentiality, bias and interpretation.

### Governance, risk and indicators

- Risk capacity, appetite, tolerance and limits are distinguished while acknowledging framework vocabulary differences.
- Safety, airworthiness, security, legal prohibition, severe rights constraints and mandatory technical authority are non-compensable gates, not weighted criteria.
- Indicator design now explicitly covers input, activity, output, outcome and impact; leading/lagging measures; denominators; boundaries; data quality; causal claims and perverse incentives.

## Terminology consistency review

The complete course was checked for the requested terms. The following controlled distinctions now apply:

- **impact** is change to people/environment; **outcome** is a resulting condition; neither is synonymous with activity or output;
- **dependency**, **impact**, **risk** and **opportunity** remain separate but connected;
- **materiality** depends on purpose; **double materiality** contains impact and financial lenses; ISSB’s investor focus is not called double materiality;
- **stakeholder** is broader than **rights-holder**;
- **metric**, **target**, **outcome**, **impact**, **claim**, **verification** and **assurance** are not interchangeable;
- **lifecycle thinking** is not formal **LCA**;
- **due diligence** is iterative and risk-based; **remedy** addresses effects on people and is not mere corrective action;
- **risk appetite**, **tolerance**, **capacity** and **limit** are defined within the selected framework, with mandatory constraints outside compensating scores.

## Residual methodological risks

1. The methods are intentionally essentials-level. Production must not imply professional qualification or completion of a formal assessment.
2. Framework language can change after the cut-off; EFRAG/ESRS, ISSB, TNFD, IAASB, chemical and climate materials need version control.
3. Legal applicability, formal thresholds, assurance engagement terms, remedies, interview protocols and consultation rights require qualified specialists.
4. Internal APS policies, authority maps, risk appetite, tolerances, grievance channels and technical thresholds were not supplied and were not invented.
5. Fictional examples need internal technical, safety, security, legal, HR, privacy, procurement and sustainability review before production.
